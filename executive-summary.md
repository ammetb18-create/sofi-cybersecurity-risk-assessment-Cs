# Executive Summary

## Project Overview

This independent cybersecurity risk assessment case study evaluates key cybersecurity and GRC risk areas relevant to SoFi Technologies, Inc., based on publicly available information and general cybersecurity risk management practices.

The purpose of this project is to demonstrate practical cybersecurity governance, risk management, control mapping, audit readiness, and executive communication skills from a GRC analyst perspective.

This case study does not represent an official assessment of SoFi Technologies, Inc. and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or commissioned by the company.

## Business Context

SoFi operates in the financial technology sector, where digital platforms, customer trust, sensitive financial data, third-party providers, regulatory expectations, and system availability are central to business operations.

Because fintech companies commonly handle personal information, financial records, account activity, authentication data, loan application information, and vendor-supported services, cybersecurity risk has direct business, legal, operational, regulatory, and reputational implications.

## Key Risk Areas Identified

The assessment identified several major cybersecurity risk areas:

1. Unauthorized access to customer personal or financial data  
2. Phishing attacks targeting employees or administrative users  
3. Account takeover affecting customer accounts  
4. Excessive employee access privileges  
5. Third-party vendor security weakness or data breach  
6. Misconfigured cloud storage exposing sensitive files  
7. Incomplete security logging or monitoring  
8. Incomplete incident response documentation or testing  
9. Insecure handling of loan application documents  
10. Weak audit evidence management  
11. Regulatory or compliance exposure due to inadequate cybersecurity governance  

## Highest Priority Risks

The highest-priority risks in this case study are:

| Risk ID | Risk Area | Rating | Business Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| R-001 | Unauthorized access to customer personal or financial data | Critical | Could affect customer trust, privacy, regulatory exposure, and financial services integrity. |
| R-002 | Phishing attack targeting employees or administrative users | Critical | Could lead to credential compromise, unauthorized access, operational disruption, or data exposure. |
| R-010 | Account takeover affecting customer accounts | Critical | Could result in fraud, unauthorized activity, customer harm, and reputational damage. |
| R-005 | Third-party vendor security weakness or data breach | High | Could create indirect customer data exposure, compliance concerns, or business disruption. |
| R-006 | Misconfigured cloud storage exposing sensitive files | High | Could expose sensitive files, loan application documents, or internal compliance records. |

## Key Control Recommendations

Based on the risk register, risk matrix, and control mapping, the following control areas should be prioritized:

- Strengthen multi-factor authentication and access control enforcement
- Implement recurring privileged access and role-based access reviews
- Improve phishing awareness, reporting, and email security controls
- Strengthen customer account takeover detection and response
- Improve third-party vendor risk management and security due diligence
- Review cloud storage permissions, encryption, and configuration monitoring
- Centralize security logging and improve audit trail completeness
- Conduct incident response tabletop exercises
- Standardize audit evidence collection and control ownership
- Maintain cybersecurity governance documentation and risk reporting

## Remediation Roadmap Summary

The recommended remediation roadmap is organized into three phases:

| Phase | Timeframe | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | 0–30 days | Strengthen MFA, access control, phishing defense, privileged access review, and customer account protection. |
| Phase 2 | 31–60 days | Improve vendor risk management, cloud security, logging, monitoring, and incident response readiness. |
| Phase 3 | 61–90 days | Strengthen offboarding, document handling, audit evidence management, and cybersecurity governance documentation. |

## Business Value

This case study demonstrates how cybersecurity risks can be translated into business-readable priorities, control recommendations, and remediation actions.

The project supports the following business goals:

- Protect customer personal and financial information
- Reduce unauthorized access and account takeover risk
- Improve audit readiness and compliance documentation
- Strengthen third-party and cloud security oversight
- Improve incident detection and response readiness
- Support executive-level cybersecurity risk communication
- Connect cybersecurity controls to business and regulatory concerns

## GRC Analyst Perspective

From a GRC analyst perspective, this project demonstrates the ability to:

- Identify cybersecurity risks in a fintech environment
- Organize risks by likelihood, impact, and priority
- Connect risks to control categories and cybersecurity frameworks
- Recommend practical remediation actions
- Communicate findings in a clear, professional, and business-friendly format
- Support audit readiness, compliance reporting, and risk ownership discussions

## Conclusion

The assessment highlights that fintech cybersecurity risk is closely connected to access control, customer data protection, third-party risk, cloud security, monitoring, incident response readiness, and governance documentation.

A structured GRC approach helps translate these risks into clear controls, priorities, ownership, and remediation steps. This type of work supports stronger cybersecurity governance, better audit readiness, and improved communication between security, compliance, technology, and business teams.
